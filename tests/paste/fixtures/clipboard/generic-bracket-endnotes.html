The Lighthouse and the Harbour Town

Near the old breakwater at Port Merrin, a small brass plate names the lookout after the town's first keeper. The keeper was identified as 'E. Hallow' in the harbourmaster's ledger for Sunday January 1, 1837.[1] The ledger describes the keeper as a careful and observant person in their mid-twenties, already well known along the coast. Besides the name and the date, the plate omits almost every detail of the story: 'The keeper was invited aboard the survey vessel (anchored in Merrin Bay) to mark the opening of the new light on 1 January 1837 following the completion of the tower in December 1836.'[2] In this way, the plate recruits the keeper into a ceremony recorded only by the visitors.

From the ledger we learn that on the same day the keeper was given a lamp and a signal flag, and asked to demonstrate the shutter mechanism to the assembled crew.[3] The demonstration was repeated twice before the tide turned.

The tower's own records tell a different story. An entry from the following week notes that the keeper had climbed the stair alone and re-trimmed every wick before dawn.[4] Whether this was duty or defiance the record does not say.[5] The question stays open in the town's retelling to this day.

[1] E. Hallow and M. Hallow, Harbour Ledger, 687.

[2] Plate, Town of Port Merrin, Keeper's Lookout, unveiled 24 September 2006.

[3] E. Hallow and M. Hallow, Harbour Ledger, 687.

[4] R. Tidewell, Sunday January 8 1837, Port Merrin, Log of the Survey Vessel Cormorant.

[5] E. Hallow and M. Hallow, Harbour Ledger, 688.
