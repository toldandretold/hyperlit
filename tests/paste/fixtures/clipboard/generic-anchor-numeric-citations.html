Estimating Informal Employment

Household surveys systematically undercount informal employment, and the size of the undercount varies with how the enumerator phrases the activity question [1]. Comparisons across countries are therefore comparisons of survey instruments as much as of labour markets.

Establishment surveys have the opposite bias: they capture the formal sector well and miss almost everything below the registration threshold [2]. Combining the two sources is standard practice and introduces its own difficulties.

The most widely used reconciliation procedure was set out two decades ago and has been revised twice since [3]. Its critics argue that the revisions have made it less transparent without making it more accurate [4].

What is not in dispute is that the resulting series are used for policy at a level of precision the underlying data cannot support.
