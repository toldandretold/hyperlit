# Peer Review Under Pressure: A Toy Commentary

## The load on editors

Editorial workloads have been a concern since long before language models. Surveys of peer review have documented rising submission volumes and reviewer fatigue across fields (Publons, 2018). The classic defence of peer review as a quality filter coexists with long-standing evidence of its unreliability; a well-known study found that agreement between independent reviewers is only modestly better than chance (Cicchetti, 1991).

Structural analyses argue that the review system's economics are strained. Aczel (2021) estimated that reviewers collectively spend over 100 million hours per year on peer review, an effort largely invisible in research assessment.

## What tooling can and cannot do

Automated screening already exists at the margins of editorial workflow, most visibly in statistics checking (Nuijten, 2016). Proposals for open review infrastructures argue that transparency redistributes rather than removes the verification burden (Ross-Hellauer, 2017).

## Conclusion

Any tool that claims to reduce editorial load must be evaluated on measured time, cost, and error rates, not on demonstrations. This toy commentary exists so a control book with verifiably intact citations can flow through the same evaluation pipeline as its corrupted sibling.
